# Operations in the window

arcform holds a catalogue of 13 operations (filter, sort, hide, rename, derive, cast, fill, dedupe, limit, summarise, join, append, read a file). Brightfield makes each one reachable in the window through the interaction that suits it, previews its result as it is made, and records it through arc on Save. This document covers the parts every operation shares, the decisions behind them, and how to add an operation.

## The ruling this follows

The owner ruled on 2026-11-22 that each operation gets the interaction that suits it. Some operations want a small form, some are typed in place on a column's header, and some ask nothing and are a direct action on what is on screen. The foot row of the grid stays what it was: a convenience for a simple `where` and a simple `order by`, and not the place every operation is asked.

His worry about "a sea of dials and dropdowns" is the settings pages of cloud consoles, not forms as such. The aim is a window that recognises and responds to what the analyst does: operations that are transparent, with sensible defaults, previewed at each keystroke as DuckDB's Instant SQL previews a query.

Two rules follow from arc holding the catalogue. Brightfield writes no operation's SQL: a preview runs the model arc writes, and the step a Save records is the one `arc operation record` writes, byte for byte what any other way of recording the same operation writes. And an operation's values, which of them are required, and its words come from `arc operation describe`. The words Brightfield adds are its own labels, menus and sentences.

## The shared parts

| Part | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| arc's description, read with its shapes | `crates/brightfield-shell/src/operation.rs` | Each value's shape (a kind of SQL, a column's name, a list of names, a whole number, one word of a few, a path, text), whether it is required, which values go together, and whether a table role takes one table or a list. `record_step` records an operation naming each table it reads by its role. |
| A slot per operation | `crates/brightfield-shell/src/operation_ui/` | One file per operation holding its `OperationUi`: where it starts, what the window must hold for it to start, the draft it begins as, whether it is kept at once or on Enter, how it asks, and how its step is named. `OPERATIONS` lists the 13 slots; a slot answers `None` until its operation is built. |
| A key per operation | `crates/brightfield-keys/src/operations.rs` | One key-registry entry per operation, each behind a `*_BUILT` constant of its own, with every key chosen up front so two operations built side by side cannot pick the same one. |
| arc's model for a draft | `operation_ui/draft.rs` | `draft_model` records the draft into a Protocol of stub tables in a temp folder, reads back the model arc wrote, and removes the folder. arc's history snapshots for that folder are sent inside it, so the analyst's history holds no entry for it. |
| The warm preview | `crates/brightfield-engine/src/preview.rs` | `WarmPreview` loads the data file and makes the steps the draft reads once, on a connection shut to anything outside it, then runs each edit's model alone. It holds the table whole, so a preview of an aggregate is exact. |
| The reads of a draft | `operation_ui/reads.rs` | One worker per draft. An edit is read at once with no pause; the worker takes the newest request waiting, so one arc child runs at a time and the last edit of a burst is always read; an answer for text since changed is dropped. |
| Keeping a draft | `crates/brightfield-shell/src/typed_sql.rs`, `window.rs` | A kept draft is an unsaved step carrying the request arc will be asked to record. It is laid out at the spine's foot and previewed by running arc's model. Save records it through arc; `remove-step` on the spine takes it back. |
| The window's side | `crates/brightfield-shell/src/window/operation_host.rs` | One entry point for a key, a palette row, a header gesture and a menu item; the keys while an operation is being made; the status band's line. |
| A header as a place to act | `operation_ui/header.rs` | The parts of a column's header an operation starts from (a double-click on the name, a click on the type or the missing count), the menu a secondary click opens, the new-column cell, and the field an operation asks with in place, with what it suggests listed under it. |
| A form in the Operation section | `crates/brightfield-shell/src/operation_panel.rs` | An operation with a form of its own draws it in the inspector's Operation section, over its own draft. The section adds the operation's name and no control of its own. |
| The spine picks a table | `crates/brightfield-shell/src/spine_pick.rs`, `window/operation_host.rs`, `protocol.rs` | An operation that reads a second table, or a list of them, asks for it in the navigator rail's spine before its draft begins. The rail takes the keys, in a key context of its own (`SpinePick` in `crates/brightfield-keys`), and the operation's `begin` is handed each table taken with its columns. |
| Availability | `operation_ui/open.rs` | What the window must hold for an operation to start, checked in the order the operation lists its needs, with one sentence per need. |
| Reading a generated step back | `crates/brightfield-shell/src/generated.rs` | A folder holding a step arc generated for any operation opens, lays out and previews. A filter or a sort is read back into its clause; any other step is read by its header and the table it makes, and its preview runs the model as arc wrote it. |

## From a start to a Save

1. **A start.** A key in the grid, a palette row, a gesture on a part of a column's header, or a menu item comes to `MeridianApp::start_operation`. It says the first thing in its way on the status band: the window has no arc, arc's description has not come, a need is unmet, or the operation's own `begin` refuses. The sentence stays until the next key or pointer press.
   An operation that starts from the spine (`Start::Spine`) opens the spine's pick here, in place of its draft, and its draft begins once a table is taken. See "The spine's pick" below.
2. **The draft.** `begin` makes the draft with its defaults filled from what Brightfield knows of the column. A column given to a value is spelled for that value: bare for a value arc takes as a name, and as SQL spells it (quoted when it needs quotes) for a value arc takes as SQL.
3. **The preview.** The draft is read at each edit. The grid shows the answer's page under the preview label with the missing-value counts of the preview's own columns. While arc or DuckDB refuses the draft as it stands, the last good rows stay on screen, faded, and the reason is on the status band.
4. **The Editor.** The Editor tab shows arc's model for the draft, read-only, following each edit. For a kept or recorded step in front it shows that step's model.
5. **Keeping.** Enter keeps the draft once each value arc requires is given and the reads hold arc's model for the draft as it stands. An operation that asks nothing is kept on its first answer. Esc drops the draft. A kept draft is an unsaved step; no file is written.
6. **Saving.** ⌘S keeps an open draft first when Enter would, then records the kept steps through arc in the order they were made. A draft that cannot be kept stays open, saying why, and the rest is saved. A refusal from arc at Save leaves the step kept, with arc's words on a banner.

Starting an operation over the grid of a kept step of the same operation corrects that step: the draft begins from what was kept, and Enter replaces it in place under its name. An operation kept at once with no form asks for nothing a correction could change, so over its own kept step it makes a new step that reads that one: a second hide hides another column. An operation kept at once with a form of its own, as dedupe is, opens its kept step in that form as soon as it is kept, and over its own kept step corrects it there; a correction is kept on Enter, never at once, and Esc leaves the step as it was kept.

## The spine's pick

An operation whose first start is `Start::Spine { role }` reads a second table under that role of arc's. Starting it opens the pick: the navigator rail takes the keys and its focus ring, and the spine is drawn for the pick until a table is taken or Esc backs out.

- The table in front trails `in front`. Each table the operation can read trails `related` when the Protocol's model holds a relationship between it and the table in front, and otherwise `shares <column>` when it holds the column the grid's cursor is on. Every row that is not one of those tables (a step, a view, the data file, the model's step) is named in the muted ink and answers no click.
- The cursor starts on the first related table, else the first that shares the cursor's column, else the first. It is drawn as the selected row.
- `j` and `k`, and the arrows, move the cursor. `Enter` takes the table under it. For a role that takes a list, `Space` adds the table under the cursor, or takes it off, and `Enter` takes the added tables in the order they were added. `Esc` gives the keys back to the grid and begins nothing. A click on a table's row puts the cursor on it. The status band names the five keys, read from the key registry.
- The tables come from one of two places. In a Protocol made from a data file they are the loaded table and the steps after it. While the grid holds a table of a Protocol made from a folder, workbook or database, they are the model's tables that have rows, which are the tables the rail lists there; a step kept on the grid's table has no row on that rail and is not offered. Each of the model's other tables is read from its grid copy, which the operation's preview loads beside the table in front.
- A table's columns are read on a worker, so the spine draws at once. A step's columns are read from a description of its table, so a step that makes no rows still says what it shares. A model table's are read from the Protocol's database with no row copied; its grid copy is written when it is taken, and for no other candidate.
- The tables the pick lists are the ones the operation's needs were checked against (`OperationContext::tables`), so `Need::AnotherTable` is met exactly when the pick has a candidate. Over a join's rows, where the rail does not draw the model's spine, the model's tables are not offered.
- Which of the model's tables a preview loads is read from the steps it runs, not from what was picked: a preview over a model table's grid loads the grid copy of each of the model's other tables that one of its steps reads, and of no other (`generated::with_file_roots`). So a step kept from a draft, and the same step saved and opened in a later session, load what the draft did. A copy the folder lacks, or one older than the Protocol's database, is written when the preview is asked for, on that frame; one that cannot be written is tried again two seconds later. While a run this window started is writing the Protocol's database, no copy is written and the pick does not open: arc frees the database between two steps, and a read that opened it then would fail the next step. A statement typed on the statement form reads a model's table where the folder holds a current copy of it.
- A saved step is previewed from the grid of the table it is on. From another table's grid, a step that reads a step kept on the first table is listed without the steps it reads, and its preview is refused; that is how a folder Protocol's steps are listed for each table, and is not the pick's to change.

The take hands `begin` the tables through `Begin::taken`, each with its columns, and the Protocol's model through `Begin::model`: the model pane's while one of its tables is in the grid, and otherwise the `model.ossie.yaml` in the Protocol's folder when it holds one. `Begin::model` is `None` for an operation that did not start from the spine.

## Decisions made on the owner's behalf

Each of these is reversible, and each is the owner's to overturn.

1. **A kept operation, other than filter and sort, is a new node**: an unsaved step at the spine's foot with the not-recorded mark and its table in front, as a statement kept from the palette is. Filter and sort stay views of the node in front.
2. **The preview runs arc's own model on each edit.** arc's round trip, about 150 ms a call when this was built, is the floor until arc can render a model in-process.
3. **Fade, don't clear.** While an edit fails, the grid keeps the last good rows faded. The rows are not faded while a newer edit is being read and has not been refused: the read takes about a tenth of a second, and a fade at each keystroke would flicker.
4. **A preview states its own missing values**, as a line above its rows naming each column that has some with its count. The header band's counts are the loaded table's, which the operation may have changed. A coloured validity band in the preview's own header would need the rest of the column profile for rows that are not profiled, so the count is given as a number.
5. **Direct actions keep at once.** An operation that asks nothing keeps its step as soon as arc admits it and DuckDB has run it, and the status band says what it did and that `remove-step`'s key on the spine takes it back: "Hid house_age · d d on the spine takes it back" for a hidden column, and "Kept" with the step's name in place of the first part for any other. A dedupe that keeps every row keeps no step and says the table has no duplicate rows. A refusal keeps nothing and leaves arc's or DuckDB's reason on the status band; there are no earlier rows to fade, since the action asked for nothing before it.
6. **Names.** `<table>_<word>_by_<column>` for row operations and `<table>_<word>_<column>` for column operations, slugged, with `_2`, `_3` for a name taken. A name says only what that one step did, and is fixed when the draft is kept. The column comes from a value read as arc reads it: a column's name given bare (`By::Name`, as hide and rename give theirs) is taken whole, so `median income` names `with_nulls_renamed_median_income`, and a value arc takes as SQL (`By::Value`) is read for a plain column and names nothing when it is an expression.
7. **Keys** take VisiData's where it has one: `-` hide, `=` derive, `f` fill, `shift-f` summarise, `&` join. The rest are `shift-r` rename, `shift-t` cast, `shift-u` dedupe, `shift-l` limit and `shift-a` append.
8. **An operation does not start over a `where` or `order by` that is applied and not recorded**, and says why: it would read the table, not the rows on screen.
9. **The Editor shows a step's SQL read-only.** Editing it there into a SQL step is later work.
10. **`remove-step` on a kept step takes it back at once**, a kept statement included, since no file holds it. A step kept after it that reads its table refuses that, named in a banner.
11. **A statement typed over a draft's grid is a step of its own.** The draft's text is arc's model, which is corrected from the operation and not typed over.
12. **A `read-file` step previews when it reads a local CSV or Parquet file**, loaded beside the data file before the preview's connection is shut. A URL, a glob, another format, or a path from the home folder is left to a run, and the preview says so.
13. **The foot row's fields open without waiting on arc, and without a Protocol.** They write their own text and apply it as a view, so `w` and `o` open their fields in a window with no arc, and in one with no Protocol of its own, where a click on the field opens it too. The palette lists the two only where a Protocol is saved.
14. **A keystroke typed into a field in place is that field's alone.** While an operation asks on a header, a letter goes into its value and reaches no other handler, so `o` typed into a name does not sort the rows. A key held with Cmd, Ctrl or Alt is still the window's.
15. **The new-column cell stays in reach.** It stands after the table's last column where that leaves it inside the pane, and at the pane's trailing edge otherwise. While an operation starts from it, the grid leaves a gutter one cell wide after its columns, so at the trailing edge the cell stands beside the last column's header rather than over it. The cell is that gutter's width, and the field it opens reaches back over the table until the new column comes.
16. **A header's type is one place, not a region.** The type part is the leaf and storage row at the full density, the title beside the name at the compact one, and the glyph where neither is drawn.
17. **A draft goes when the grid it began over does.** Its keys and its preview are that grid's. Once the canvas holds another view or the graph, or a field of the foot row takes the keyboard, the draft is dropped as Esc drops it, so typing never goes to a draft that is no longer on screen.

18. **The pick's candidates stay in the spine's order.** A related table is where the cursor starts, and it is not moved to the head of the list, so `j` and `k` move down and up the rail as it is drawn.
19. **A table says `shares` only for the column the grid's cursor is on.** A table that shares some other column says nothing; the operation's own defaults find the first column both tables hold.
20. **The cursor follows the columns until a key moves it.** A step's columns arrive a moment after the pick opens, so the cursor's starting row is settled as they come, and it stays where a key or a click put it.
21. **Enter waits for the columns of the table it takes.** While they are still being read the status band says to press Enter again, since `begin` proposes a join's keys from them.
22. **An added table trails `added`** ahead of what it shares, and is drawn selected as the cursor's row is. The design system has no second row mark to tell the two apart, and none was added.
23. **Space on a pick for one table adds nothing and says so**, in place of being ignored or opening the palette.
24. **The pick closes, beginning nothing, when its grid leaves the canvas, another pane takes the keys or the rail is put away**, as a draft goes when the grid it began over does.
25. **A model's table is copied when it is taken, on the frame of the take**, as opening a table's grid copies it. Reading the columns of each candidate copies nothing.
26. **A kept step being corrected skips the pick.** It holds its tables already, and its draft begins from them.
27. **A field in place opens with its value selected whole**, as a name field in a file browser does. The first text typed replaces it and Backspace empties it, while an arrow, Home or End lets go of it with the caret after it; after that the typing adds to the end. Tab to another value selects that one whole. A rename's field opens on the column's own name, so typing a new name needs no clearing first, and a field that opens empty has nothing selected. The selection is drawn under the editor's selection wash, with no caret.
28. **A value can follow another one.** An in-place field's `follows` keeps a value in step with the one being typed: a derived column's name follows its expression while it is still the name the expression before the edit gave, so a name typed into, or one kept from a correction under another name, stays. The name is the expression's letters, digits and `_`, each run of anything else made one `_`, trimmed and cut at 30 characters, then made free of the table's columns with `_2`, `_3`; an expression with no letter or digit names it `derived`.
29. **A derived column's field moves over its column.** It opens in the new-column cell, and once the preview holds the new column it is drawn over that column's header, so the values fill under it as the expression is typed.
30. **Derive column needs no column.** The new-column cell starts it on none, with an empty expression. From `=` on a cell or "Add a column from this one" on a header's menu, the expression starts as that column as SQL spells it.
31. **Derive column completes the column the expression's last word begins**, as SQL spells it, quoted where it needs quotes, with the rest of the expression kept. Down picks one and Tab takes it. The name, once in focus, offers nothing.
32. **Fill nulls opens on a fill its storage type takes**, selected whole: `0` for a number, `'unknown'` for text, `false` for a boolean, and nothing for any other type. For a number it also offers the column's median, rounded to a whole number on an integer column so that taking it keeps the column's type. What it offers leaves out the value as it stands.
33. **Fill nulls starts only over the loaded table's grid.** Which columns have missing values is known there alone, so over a step's preview, a kept fill's included, it says it needs a column with missing values. A kept fill is changed by taking it back with `remove-step` and filling again.
34. **Cast column opens on the type FineType reads, when it is not the type the column is stored as and a plain cast reaches it.** That is the DuckDB type FineType's transforms give the column's label, selected whole, so Enter keeps it and typing replaces it. FineType reaches some types through a format, such as a `strptime` for a day-first date in a file too large to be cast at open, and arc's `cast-column` is a plain `CAST`, which those values fail; such a label is not the default. A column with no label, one whose label reads as its storage type, one whose type FineType reaches through a format, or a step's column the window holds no profile for, opens empty, and Enter on it says `cast-column needs type`.
35. **Cast column offers FineType's type, then `VARCHAR`, `BIGINT`, `DOUBLE`, `DECIMAL(18, 3)`, `DATE`, `TIMESTAMP` and `BOOLEAN`, less the type the column is stored as**, each once, compared in upper case with spaces dropped. The whole list is offered while the field is empty or holds FineType's type as it opened, and once anything else is typed, the types that contain it. A type taken from the list replaces the value whole. The menu row reads "Change type", and the column is given bare, since arc takes it as a name, so a step's name holds it whole (`with_nulls_cast_median_income`).

36. **Limit rows opens on ten rows, largest first, by the column it started on**: `count` is `10` and `order_by` is the column as SQL spells it with `desc`, so the preview is already an answer and Enter keeps it. It is the first operation to ask in a form in the Operation section: the count, the direction as the two words *largest first* and *smallest first*, and the order as text, for an order over several columns. The step is named for its count (`california_housing_sample_first_11`), since the order may be an expression.
37. **The direction words set the direction of the order's first item** and keep the rest as typed: the word's sign replaces an `asc` or `desc` the item has, goes before a `nulls` where it has one, and is added at its end otherwise. An item with no direction reads as smallest first, as SQL orders it. Items are split, and their words read, only outside quotes and parentheses, so `round(median_income, 1) desc` is one item ordered largest first, and a quoted name holding `desc` is no direction.
38. **The count field holds the keyboard while nothing else does**, so Up and Down change the count from the moment the form opens: by one, and by ten with Shift, never below 0. A count that is not a whole number, such as `ten`, is left as typed by Up and Down, and arc's refusal is on the status band.
39. **Summarise rows begins as a count of each of the column's values**, as VisiData's frequency table does, and asks nothing to begin: `group_by` is the column as SQL spells it and `aggregates` is `count(*) AS n`, so Enter keeps it as it opened.
40. **Summarise rows' form lists each select list twice: as chips, and as the text arc records.** A group's chip takes it off with a click; *+ group* offers the columns not grouped by, a category's or a date's first (by its chart tile, or a `datetime.` label). A value whose item is one function of one column carries that function as a chip of its own, which offers `avg`, `sum`, `min`, `max` and `count_distinct`; its name follows the function while it is still the name the function gave. *+ value* offers one item per column not grouped by, a number's first: `avg` for a decimal, `sum` for an integer and `count(DISTINCT …)` for anything else, each named `<function>_<column>`. Only a column stored as a number is averaged or summed, since FineType labels some text a number (`45%`, `1,5`) that DuckDB cannot average; on one, FineType's label decides between the two, a label that is not a number (a year) counts it distinct, and without a label the storage type decides. A value's name, the count's `n` included, is made free with `_2`, `_3` of every other value's and of the columns grouped by, compared without case as DuckDB compares them. Anything typed into the text stands as typed, and arc's refusal of it (a constant grouped by, for one) is said on the status band with the last good rows faded.
41. **Dedupe rows keeps `SELECT DISTINCT *` at once, then opens its form over the kept step.** `shift-u`, or "Remove duplicate rows" on a header's menu, asks nothing, and the step is named `<table>_deduped`. The form in the Operation section refines it: "One row per" lists the table's columns, a click putting one in the key or taking it out, kept in the table's order; "The first by" is the order, typed. The order is the key's first column, descending, until it is typed over, since arc takes `key` and `order_by` together or not at all. The step keeps the name it was kept under when the key is set, since a name is fixed when a draft is kept.
42. **Append rows asks the spine for its tables, then shows how their columns match.** `shift-a`, or "Append rows of other tables" on a header's menu, opens the pick; `Space` adds each table in the order its rows will follow and Enter takes them. The Operation section then has a row per column name, in the order `UNION ALL BY NAME` gives the result (the table in front's columns, then each name a later table adds, matched without regard to case as DuckDB matches), and a column per table under its number (the tables are numbered above it, so the form fits the rail), each cell *✓* or *null*. It asks nothing more, and Enter keeps `<table>_appended`. Started over its own kept step it opens as a correction, whose record names the tables it reads but not their columns, and the columns in front are the appended result's, so the form lists the tables and says their columns are not read there, with no grid.
43. **A table that shares no column's name with the table in front is dimmed in an append's pick** and trails `shares no column`. The cursor does not start on it, and Space or Enter on it says it shares no column. Once every table's columns are read and none shares one, the pick closes with "Append rows needs a second table with matching columns." A step's columns are not known when availability is checked, so that sentence comes from the pick rather than from the need.
44. **An operation whose first start is the spine asks the spine from any start**: its key, a palette row, or a header's menu. The column it started on is kept for its draft.
45. **Join tables opens the spine's pick from each of its starts**: `&` on a cell, "Join on this column" on a header's menu, and the palette. Once a table is taken, the keys are proposed from the Protocol's model, then from the column the operation started on, then from the first of the table's columns the other holds, each compared without case as DuckDB compares names. A relationship on the same names on both sides joins `using` them, and one on different names is a `condition`, `<table>.<a> = <other>.<b>` for each pair, joined by `AND`, with each table and column spelled as SQL spells it. Two tables sharing no column begin with no key, and arc's refusal says the join needs one until a condition is typed. `kind` is left to arc's default, `left`.
46. **Join tables' form holds the kind, the keys, the prefix and the join read as SQL.** The kind is arc's seven words. The keys are two fields, "on the columns both hold" (`using`, names given bare) and "or where" (`condition`, SQL); typing into one empties the other, since arc takes exactly one. The prefix is shown as `<other>_`, arc's default, until one is typed, and is not offered on a `semi` or `anti` join, which keeps the table's columns alone and refuses a prefix; choosing one of those empties a prefix typed. Choosing `cross` keeps the keys as they were, and arc's refusal says a cross join takes none. Under the fields a line reads the join as SQL, such as `left join by_age using (house_age)`, with the count of the columns the preview adds ("Adds 1 column") while the join is first made; a kept join being corrected has its own rows in front rather than the table joined, so it shows no count. The count is drawn in the form rather than on the status band, which holds an operation's refusals. The step is named for the table joined, `<table>_joined_<other>`, through a name rule that reads the table a draft reads under a role (`By::Read`).

## What arc lacks

These are arcform's to close. Until then the workarounds above stand.

1. **A render that writes nothing, in-process.** Each edit records into a throwaway Protocol through a child process. A library call that returns the model for a request, with arc's refusals and no folder, would close most of the gap to Instant SQL and retire the stub tables.
2. **A record of a step's operation that a program can read.** A generated step carries its model and a header written for a person. Brightfield reads the tables a step reads from that header, which is ambiguous for a table whose name holds a comma and a space. A header of two names that are one table is settled by the model's own quoted spelling; three or more names with two of them one table is not. Correcting a recorded step from its form is out of reach for the same reason.
3. **Which column values must already exist.** A column-shaped value does not say whether it must be a column of the table or is a new name, so each operation's `begin` says it.
4. **A list item holding a comma.** The command line splits a list on `,`.
5. **Rules the schema does not state.** `join-tables` takes exactly one of `using` or `condition`, which only arc's check enforces, so a form learns it from the refusal.
6. **No stage, and no other tool's word, in a description.** Brightfield holds the stages.

## Adding an operation

An operation is three files, so operations can be built side by side.

1. **Fill its slot** in `crates/brightfield-shell/src/operation_ui/<op>.rs`: a `static` `OperationUi` and a `ui()` that returns it. Say where it starts (`starts`), what it needs (`needs`, in the order the sentences should be said), the draft it begins as (`begin`), whether it keeps at once or on Enter, its in-place field or its form, and its name rule.
2. **Flip its `*_BUILT`** in `crates/brightfield-keys/src/operations.rs`. Its key then joins the registry, the palette and the help sheet. `hide-column` and `derive-column` use `-` and `=`, which the Workspace binds to zooming; a dispatch test in `crates/brightfield-keys` holds each to its operation in the grid's context. A shifted key is taken before its bare letter in the grid, so `shift-l` limits rows and `l` still moves the cursor.
3. **Write its test file** from the harness in `crates/brightfield-shell/tests/support/operations.rs`: an `OpWindow` on a copy of a fixture, started from the operation's key, its header gesture and the palette.

The tests in `tests/operation_registry.rs` then bite: the slot holds its own operation, its verb is in the key registry, its draft names only what arc describes, its needs agree with what arc says it reads, and it is filed under its stage.

An operation that reads a second table lists `Start::Spine { role }` first in its `starts`, with the role arc reads that table under, and declares `Need::AnotherTable` (or `Need::AnotherTableSharingAColumn`) among its needs. Its `begin` then finds the tables in `Begin::taken`. Its test starts it with `start_operation_spec` and reads the pick through `MeridianApp::spine_pick`, as `tests/spine_pick.rs` does.

An operation that is kept at once and has a form (`keeps: Keeps::AtOnce` with `form: Some(..)`) needs nothing more: the window opens its kept step in the form as a correction.

One thing the shared code does not do yet. The palette lists an unavailable operation without greying it, and choosing it says its sentence; a header's menu does grey it, with the sentence under it.
