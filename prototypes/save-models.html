Opening and saving, two ways

Drive opening and saving under two save models and watch, after every step, what the front door says, what is written where, and what survives a quit. A throwaway prototype: the data is invented and nothing touches your disk.

How: pick a scenario, press its highlighted step (or do the same thing in the window), and flip the switch at any point to replay your steps under the other model. ⌘S, ⌘↩ and ⌘Z work.

Save model

Is this switch macOS's own “Ask to keep changes when closing documents” setting? Partly.

Apple's guidelines describe that setting, in Desktop & Dock, as turning autosave off. With it on, a document shows a dot on its close button and Edited in its title, and closing or quitting asks you to save. That is “A draft until saved”. With it off, macOS autosaves and keeps versions you can go back to with Revert To, which is the heart of “Brightfield keeps it”. The difference: macOS autosaves the document itself, wherever it lives, while “Brightfield keeps it” keeps it in Brightfield's own folder and writes the folder copy only on ⌘S. So the switch is that setting's two sides, plus a choice about where the kept copy lives, which macOS doesn't make.

Apple's default is autosave: avoid making people save explicitly, save periodically while they edit and when they close. Its File menu, in order, has Open…, Open Recent, Close, Save, Duplicate (preferred over Save As), Rename…, Move To… and Revert To. The window's File menu below follows that order.
